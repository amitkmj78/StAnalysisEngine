1. Purpose and principles

Turn the app from "a model that makes predictions" into a research tool people trust: every signal is explained, every past call is checkable, and every page answers a real question about a stock or a portfolio.

  • Explain, don't just predict. Every score shows what drove it.
  • Show the record, good and bad. Live results are never mixed with backtests, and misses are shown.
  • Probabilities, not promises. Signals show confidence and horizon; nothing says a stock "will" go up.
  • Always compare to the simple option. Results are shown against the S&P 500.
  • No look-ahead. Every score and backtest uses only data available on that date.

4. Track record metrics

Computed for every prediction ever published, grouped by horizon (10, 30, 60, 90 days) and signal type; a prediction is scored only after its horizon ends.

MetricDefinitionShown as
Hit rateShare of predictions where the direction was right (Buy → stock rose, Trim → stock fell or lagged)% with sample size, e.g. "58% of 1,240"
Average returnMean total return of the stock over the horizon, by signal% per signal type
Excess returnStock return minus S&P 500 (SPY) return over the same windowpts, with the share of predictions that beat SPY
Rank correlation (IC)Correlation between the score's ranking of stocks and their actual return rankingMonthly value and 12-month average
CalibrationFor each confidence bucket (50–60%, 60–70%…), the actual hit rateChart: stated confidence vs actual
Worst missesLargest losses on Buy signals and largest gains on Trim signalsTop 10 list, never hidden
Model portfolioEqual-weight portfolio of Buy signals, rebalanced at each signal date, after assumed trading costsGrowth of $10,000 vs SPY
  • Predictions are stored at publish time and are append-only; nothing is edited or deleted.
  • Groups with fewer than 30 scored predictions show "not enough data yet" instead of a percentage.
  • A model version change starts a new record; older versions stay visible.

5. How explanations work

Each score is decomposed into factor contributions so users see why, in plain words.

  1. Contributions: for tree-based models, compute SHAP values per feature; for linear or rules-based scores, use weight × standardized value.
  2. Group: roll features up into 6–8 understandable factor groups: Momentum, Earnings revisions, Earnings surprise, Valuation, Quality, Growth, Volatility, Market regime.
  3. Rank: show the top 3 positive and top 2 negative groups, with a bar showing size and direction.
  4. Words: each group has a sentence template filled with the real value, e.g. "Analysts raised next-year earnings estimates 6% in 30 days."
  5. Change: show what moved the score since last week ("Score fell from 72 to 61, mostly Valuation after a 15% rise").

8. Disclosures and compliance

Questions to confirm with a securities lawyer; not legal advice.
  • Label signals as general research, not personal advice; the health check and AI answers must avoid telling a specific user to buy or sell a specific security unless the adviser question is resolved.
  • Label backtests and the model portfolio as hypothetical, with methodology, cost assumptions and limitations next to every chart.
  • Show live track record separately from backtests, including losses and worst misses.
  • Display "Past performance doesn't predict future results" wherever returns are shown.
  • Respect data licenses: real-time quotes, transcripts and news are shown under each vendor's display terms.

10. Open questions

  • Which fundamentals and estimates vendor, and does its license allow display to users?
  • Is the track record public (no login) as a marketing asset, or for signed-in users only?
  • Which model versions count toward the live record, and from what start date?
  • Free vs paid: which features are premium (e.g. AI questions, stress tests, advanced screener)?
  • Will scores expand beyond the S&P 500 (mid-caps, ETFs as first-class)?
