1. Purpose and scope

Let users act on the app's signals by placing real trades through a regulated partner broker, without the app becoming a broker-dealer. The partner holds accounts and cash, executes, clears and settles; the app owns the research, the order ticket, the experience and its own audit trail.

In scope (first release): US-listed stocks and ETFs; market, limit, stop and stop-limit orders; fractional and dollar-amount orders where the broker supports them; regular and extended hours; paper trading. Later: options, crypto, recurring buys, automated strategies. Out of scope: holding customer money, building an exchange connection or clearing.

2. Integration model

Pick one model before build; it decides onboarding, branding and compliance scope.

Recommendation: start with model A for one broker plus paper trading, and move to model B once the signal track record and user demand justify it.

3. Order flow and states

Signal or search→Order ticket→App pre-trade checks→Review and confirm→Broker API (with idempotency key)→Broker risk checks and routing→Fills streamed back→Positions and history sync

The app keeps its own order record and maps broker statuses to these states:

DRAFTTicket being filled in; nothing sent
SUBMITTINGSent to broker, awaiting acknowledgment
OPENAccepted by broker, working
PARTIALLY_FILLEDSome quantity executed
FILLEDFully executed
PENDING_CANCEL / REPLACEChange requested, not yet confirmed
CANCELLEDCancelled by user, broker or expiry
REJECTEDRefused by the app or broker, with reason
UNKNOWNNo confirmation after a timeout; reconciled with the broker before retrying

5. Non-functional requirements

IDPriorityQualityRequirement
NFR-1MustLatencyApp adds under 300 ms (p95) between Confirm and the broker API call; status updates reach the screen within 2 s of the broker event
NFR-2MustCorrectnessNo duplicate orders: every submit carries an idempotency key; retries only after checking the broker for the key
NFR-3MustAvailability99.9% during market hours; if the broker is down, trading is disabled with a clear banner and nothing is queued silently
NFR-4MustSecurityNever store broker passwords; OAuth tokens encrypted at rest with KMS; step-up authentication (passkey or MFA) to place or change orders; session timeout 15 min on trading screens
NFR-5MustReconciliationDaily and on-demand reconciliation of orders, fills and positions with the broker; mismatches alert operations
NFR-6MustRecordsKeep every order event, ticket snapshot, quote shown and disclosure accepted, immutable, for at least 6 years (confirm with counsel)
NFR-7ShouldRate limitsRespect broker API limits with per-user and global throttling; never drop an order because of throttling without telling the user
NFR-8ShouldObservabilityMetrics for submit latency, rejects by reason, UNKNOWN orders, token refresh failures; alert on spikes

6. Compliance considerations

This section flags questions for a securities lawyer; it is not legal advice. The answers can change the product, especially anything that looks like personalized advice or automated trading.
TopicWhy it mattersDesign response
Broker-dealer registrationHandling orders or customer money can require registrationPartner broker is broker of record; the app never holds funds; confirm the partner agreement covers the app's role
Investment adviser statusSignals tailored to a person's portfolio, or trading on their behalf, can make the app an adviserUser-initiated orders only; signals are general, not personalized advice; no auto-trading until counsel approves (may require RIA registration)
Performance claimsShowing backtested or hypothetical returns is restricted in marketingLabel backtests as hypothetical, show live track record separately, include methodology and risks
Pattern day trading and marginBroker enforces day-trading and margin rules; FINRA has proposed changes to the day-trading ruleShow the broker's day-trade count and warnings; rely on the broker's current rule rather than hard-coding it
Market data licensingDisplaying real-time exchange data requires licensed feedsUse the broker's or a licensed vendor's feed under its display terms; mark delayed data as delayed
Privacy and securityFinancial data falls under privacy and data-security rulesPrivacy notice, consent for account linking, data minimization, breach response plan

8. Open questions

  • Which broker first, and model A (connect your broker) or model B (accounts under your brand)?
  • US stocks and ETFs only at launch, or options too? Options need approval levels and more disclosures.
  • Will signals ever be personalized to a user's holdings? This drives the adviser question.
  • Is automated or scheduled trading on the roadmap, and when?
  • Which market data provider for real-time quotes on the ticket?
